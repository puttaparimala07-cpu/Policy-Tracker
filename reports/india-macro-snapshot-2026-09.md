## 6. Analysis

1. India's headline CPI accelerated to 4.82% in August 2026 from 4.45% in July, driven primarily by a jump in food prices (CFPI at 5.95%), moving inflation further from the RBI's 4% mid-point target but still within its 2–6% tolerance band.
2. Wholesale inflation (WPI) surged to a multi-year high of 9.92%, with fuel & power inflation at 22.93% signalling that global energy price pressures are now feeding through India's pipeline prices well ahead of retail CPI.
3. The divergence between WPI (9.92%) and CPI (4.82%) points to producers currently absorbing much of the input-cost shock rather than fully passing it to consumers — a margin squeeze that bears watching in coming prints.
4. Industrial output growth moderated to 6.7% in July from 7.3% in June, with mining contracting 0.9% even as manufacturing (7.3%) and electricity (8.7%) stayed robust — momentum remains broad-based despite the mining drag.
5. India's external trade showed strong headline growth (exports +26% YoY) but the merchandise deficit widened to $26.86 billion; a robust services surplus ($17.45 billion) continued to substantially offset the goods gap, keeping the combined deficit contained at $9.41 billion.
6. The labour market improved incrementally, with the PLFS unemployment rate easing to 5.0% in August, a six-month low, aided by falling rural joblessness — a positive signal alongside otherwise mixed price data.
7. The RBI held the repo rate at 5.25% for a fourth straight review in August, maintaining its neutral stance as it weighs rising inflation risk from global energy prices against a firm growth outlook; the October 5–7 meeting will be closely watched for any shift in tone.
8. Globally, both the Fed (to 3.75–4.00%) and the ECB (deposit rate to 2.50%) delivered rate hikes in September — a rare synchronized tightening move — as elevated crude oil prices tied to Middle East tensions pushed inflation higher across the US (3.4%) and euro area (3.2%), while China's price pressures remain comparatively muted (CPI 0.8%) and the PBoC continues to hold rates steady to support growth.
